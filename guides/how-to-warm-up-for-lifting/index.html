How to warm up before lifting weights

By Jesse Sissala · Updated November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

To warm up before lifting, do 5–10 minutes of light cardio and dynamic movements, then perform 3–6 ramp-up sets of your first exercise with gradually heavier weights and fewer reps.

A good warm-up does two jobs. It raises your body temperature and gets your joints moving, and it rehearses the exact movement you are about to load heavily. The first part is general. The second part, the ramp-up sets, is where most lifters either skip too much or do far too much.

This guide gives you a simple routine that takes about 10–15 minutes, a worked example for a 100 kg squat, and a clear answer on stretching.

Why warming up matters

A 2010 meta-analysis by Fradkin, Zazryn and Smoliga reviewed 32 high-quality studies on warming up. Performance improved after a warm-up in 79% of the measures examined, and there was little evidence that warming up was harmful.

For lifting specifically, a warm-up helps in three practical ways:

  • Warmer muscles contract faster and feel less stiff, so your first working set does not feel like a shock.
  • Movement rehearsal lets you groove technique with light loads before the weight is heavy enough to punish mistakes.
  • A readiness check. The ramp-up tells you how you feel today. If 60% feels unusually heavy, you can adjust your working weights before you start.

Step 1: general warm-up (5 minutes)

Start with about 5 minutes of easy cardio. The aim is to feel warm and breathe a little harder, not to get tired.

  • Rowing machine, bike or brisk incline walk at an easy pace.
  • Jumping jacks or skipping rope if you train at home.
  • In a cold gym or early in the morning, stretch it to 8–10 minutes.

You should be able to hold a conversation. If you are out of breath, slow down.

Step 2: dynamic mobility (3–5 minutes)

Next, move the joints you are about to train through a full range of motion. Pick 3–4 movements and do 8–10 controlled reps of each.

Dynamic warm-up moves by session type
SessionMovements
Lower bodyBodyweight squats, walking lunges, leg swings, glute bridges
Upper bodyArm circles, band pull-aparts, scapular push-ups, band dislocates
Full bodyBodyweight squats, inchworms, band pull-aparts, hip hinges with a dowel

Keep the movements smooth and controlled. You are moving joints through range, not holding long stretches.

Step 3: ramp-up sets

Ramp-up sets, also called warm-up sets, are the most important part of a lifting warm-up. You perform your first exercise with gradually heavier loads until you reach your working weight. Follow three rules:

  1. Start light. Begin with the empty bar or about 40% of your working weight.
  2. Reps go down as weight goes up. Use 8–10 reps on the lightest set and 1–2 reps on the heaviest.
  3. Do not create fatigue. Warm-up sets should feel easy. If they leave you breathing hard, you are doing too many reps.

The heavier your working weight, the more ramp-up sets you need. A working set at 50 kg might need two warm-up sets, while 180 kg might need six. Rest about 1 minute between the light sets and 1–2 minutes before the first working set.

Worked example: warming up for a 100 kg squat

Say today's plan is 3 sets of 5 at 100 kg (about 220 lb). Here is a ramp-up that prepares you without wasting energy.

For later exercises in the same session, you are already warm. One or two ramp-up sets at about 50% and 75% of the working weight are usually enough.

Common warm-up mistakes

  • Too many reps on warm-up sets. Sets of 10 at 80% of your working weight are training sets, not warm-ups. Keep reps low as the bar gets heavy.
  • Big jumps straight to the working weight. Going from the empty bar to 100 kg skips the rehearsal. Use at least two or three steps in between.
  • Long rests that cool you down. Twenty minutes of scrolling between the warm-up and the first working set undoes much of the benefit.
  • Skipping the warm-up on light days. Even a high-rep accessory day goes better after a few minutes of movement and one or two lighter sets.

How long should a warm-up take?

For most sessions, 10–15 minutes in total is plenty: about 5 minutes of general warm-up, 3–5 minutes of mobility and 5 minutes of ramp-up sets.

  • Short on time? Cut the general warm-up to 2–3 minutes, but keep your ramp-up sets. They matter most.
  • Heavy day or max attempt? Add one or two extra ramp-up singles and rest longer between them.
  • Light, high-rep day? One or two ramp-up sets may be enough.

Should you stretch before lifting?

Dynamic stretching, the controlled movements in step 2, is a good choice before lifting. A review by Behm and Chaouachi (2011) recommended the same order this guide uses: light aerobic activity, then dynamic stretching, then movements specific to your sport. Long static stretches are a different matter.

A 2016 systematic review by Behm and colleagues found that static stretching held for 60 seconds or more per muscle reduced performance immediately afterward by about 4.6% on average, while holds under 60 seconds reduced it by only about 1%. Dynamic stretching produced small improvements. In the studies where stretching was followed by other dynamic warm-up activities, no clear effect on performance was seen.

  • If a joint feels tight, short static stretches of 15–30 seconds before your ramp-up sets are fine.
  • Save longer static stretching for after your workout or a separate session, if you want to improve flexibility.
  • Always follow any stretching with ramp-up sets before heavy lifting.

If you have an injury, joint condition or other health concern, check with a doctor or physical therapist about the warm-up and exercises that suit you.

Frequently asked questions

Do warm-up sets count as working sets?

No. Warm-up sets are too light to count toward your weekly volume. See our guide on how many sets per muscle for what does count.

Is cardio enough as a warm-up for lifting?

Cardio raises your temperature, but it does not rehearse the lift. Always add ramp-up sets of your first exercise before your working sets.

How many warm-up sets should I do?

Usually 2–6, depending on how heavy your working weight is. Lighter working weights need fewer steps; heavy squats and deadlifts need more.

Should I warm up before every exercise?

Do a full ramp-up for your first exercise. For later exercises, one or two lighter sets are usually enough, especially if they train the same muscles.
