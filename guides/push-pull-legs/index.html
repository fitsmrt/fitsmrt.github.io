Push pull legs split: 3-day and 6-day PPL guide

By Jesse Sissala · Updated November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

A push pull legs split groups your training into pushing muscles (chest, shoulders, triceps), pulling muscles (back, biceps) and legs, run either once a week over 3 days or twice a week over 6 days.

Push pull legs, often shortened to PPL, is one of the most popular ways to organize gym training. It groups muscles that work together, so each session has a clear purpose and the muscles you trained yesterday get to rest today.

This guide explains both the 3-day and 6-day versions, gives you sample workouts, shows how much weekly volume each muscle gets, and helps you decide between PPL and an upper lower split.

What is a push pull legs split?

PPL sorts exercises by movement. Pushing movements move weight away from your body, pulling movements bring it toward you, and leg day covers everything below the waist.

Muscles trained on each PPL day
DayPrimary musclesTypical lifts
PushChest, front and side delts, tricepsBench press, overhead press, dips, lateral raises
PullLats, upper back, rear delts, bicepsRows, pull-ups, face pulls, curls
LegsQuads, hamstrings, glutes, calvesSquats, Romanian deadlifts, leg press, calf raises

Because the triceps and front delts already work hard during pressing, and the biceps during rows and pull-ups, grouping them on the same day means you finish them off with a few isolation sets instead of hitting them fresh on a separate day.

The 3-day PPL schedule

The 3-day version runs each workout once per week, for example push on Monday, pull on Wednesday and legs on Friday. Each muscle gets one session per week, so those sessions carry a fairly high number of sets.

This works for people who can only train three days and like longer, focused sessions. The drawback is frequency: research by Schoenfeld and colleagues found that training a muscle at least twice per week tends to produce more growth than once per week, mainly because it is easier to accumulate quality volume across two sessions. If muscle gain is your top priority on three days, a full body routine usually gives you more frequency.

On a 3-day PPL, keep each session to about 15 to 20 hard sets. Beyond that, the quality of your last few exercises usually drops off sharply.

The 6-day PPL schedule

The 6-day version runs the rotation twice, so every muscle is trained twice per week. Use two variations of each day (A and B) to vary exercises and rep ranges.

6-day push pull legs split
DaySessionEmphasis
MondayPush AHeavy bench press, 5–8 reps
TuesdayPull AHeavy rows, 6–10 reps
WednesdayLegs AHeavy squats, 5–8 reps
ThursdayPush BOverhead press and incline work, 8–12 reps
FridayPull BVertical pulling, 8–12 reps
SaturdayLegs BHinge and single-leg work, 8–12 reps
SundayRestFull rest

Six days is a big commitment. It suits intermediate and advanced lifters who recover well, sleep enough and enjoy training most days. If you regularly miss sessions, a 4-day upper lower split will likely get you better results than an unfinished 6-day plan.

For the B days on a 6-day plan, swap in different variations: seated dumbbell shoulder press, dips and cable flyes for Push B; weighted pull-ups, chest-supported rows, rear delt flyes and incline curls for Pull B; trap bar deadlifts, Bulgarian split squats, hip thrusts, leg extensions and seated calf raises for Legs B.

Weekly volume per muscle

A 2017 meta-analysis found that 10 or more weekly sets per muscle were associated with greater muscle growth than fewer than 5 sets. Here is roughly what the sample program delivers in direct sets, assuming the B days mirror the A days in set count:

Two things stand out. The 3-day version leaves several muscles under 10 direct sets, though compound lifts add indirect work for arms and shoulders. The 6-day version can push some muscles to the high end, so if you are not recovering, trim B days to 2 to 3 sets per exercise. Read how many sets per muscle for a fuller breakdown.

Progression and recovery on PPL

Use double progression: stay at a weight until you reach the top of the rep range on every set, then add about 5 lb (2.5 kg) to upper-body lifts or 5 to 10 lb (2.5 to 5 kg) to lower-body lifts. Track every session so you know exactly what to beat next time.

  • Find starting weights with the one-rep max calculator rather than guessing.
  • On a 6-day plan, sleep and food become limiting factors quickly. Aim for 7 or more hours of sleep and enough protein and calories to support your goal.
  • Plan a lighter week every 6 to 8 weeks, or sooner if lifts stall for two sessions in a row. The deload week guide explains how.

PPL vs upper lower: which should you choose?

The best split is the one that matches how many days you can reliably train.

Choosing between PPL and upper lower
SituationBetter choiceWhy
3 days a weekFull body or 3-day PPLFull body gives more frequency; PPL gives longer focused sessions
4 days a weekUpper lowerEach muscle trained twice with shorter sessions
5 days a weekUpper lower plus one day, or rolling PPLBoth work; rolling PPL trains each muscle about 1.7 times weekly
6 days a week6-day PPLTwice-weekly frequency with manageable session length
Inconsistent scheduleFull bodyMissing a day still leaves every muscle trained

A rolling PPL simply runs push, pull, legs, rest and repeats, so the training days drift through the week. It is a good middle ground if five or six fixed days do not suit you.

Frequently asked questions

Is push pull legs good for beginners?

The 3-day version is fine for beginners, but most new lifters progress faster on two or three full body sessions per week because each muscle gets trained more often. PPL becomes more useful once you need more volume per muscle.

How many exercises should a PPL day have?

Five or six is typical: two or three compound lifts followed by two or three isolation exercises. That usually adds up to 15 to 20 working sets.

Can I do PPL 5 days a week?

Yes. Run push, pull, legs, push, pull one week and continue the rotation the next, or use a rolling schedule with a rest day after every three sessions.

Is PPL better than an upper lower split?

Not inherently. When weekly volume is similar, results are similar. Choose PPL for three or six days a week and upper lower for four.
